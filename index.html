Boundary: arithmetic only Heatsink Math works out junction temperatures, heatsink budgets and dissipation limits from the numbers you type. It treats each thermal resistance as fixed, so it has not seen your build and does not know the mounting, the airflow, the interface material or the part variant, and it gives no verdict that a cooling setup is adequate or safe. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: 10 W on a three-link thermal chain, the heatsink a 150 °C junction budget calls for, and the most that setup can dissipate.

The junction truth

10 W through 11.5 °C/W junction-to-air rises 115 °C; from a 25 °C ambient the junction sits at 140 °C.

The heatsink truth

To hold a 150 °C junction over a 25 °C ambient at 10 W, the total budget is 12.5 °C/W; after 2.5 junction-to-case and 0.5 case-to-heatsink, the heatsink must be at most 9.5 °C/W.

The headroom truth

With 11.5 °C/W total junction-to-air and 125 °C of rise to spend, the part can dissipate at most 10.87 W.
